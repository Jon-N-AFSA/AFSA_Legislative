The Mission

Defending Quality of Life Since 1961

In understanding our Nation's current fiscal situation, success will not always be defined by what is gained. Success is also determined by the level at which a benefit is preserved — or by winning a portion of our objective.

AFSA is a federally chartered Military and Veterans' Service Organization representing the professional and personal interests of our members, Total Force, and their families. We work in partnership with our nation's Congressional and Military leaders on Capitol Hill, and within the DoD and VA, toward one core mission: improving the quality of life and supporting the well-being of all military personnel and their families.

Focus Group 1

Pay, Benefits & Programs

Maintain pay comparability and protect non-pay quality-of-life benefits.

Tap the ★ next to any priority to add it to your personal talking-points list — then copy it for calls, emails, and chapter meetings.

  • Support full military pay raises in line with current law.
  • Protect and strengthen BAH, COLA, special pays, leave policies, commissaries, exchanges, and MWR programs.
  • Improve Regular Military Compensation to reflect real-world economic conditions.
  • Expand bereavement leave and allow voluntary leave transfer between service members.
  • Strengthen oversight of privatized military housing and enforce the tenant bill of rights.
Focus Group 2

Veteran & Retiree Healthcare and Benefits

Honor service by delivering timely, comprehensive, and equitable care.

  • Improve access to VA healthcare, reduce claims backlogs, and fully fund ancillary support programs.
  • Extend concurrent receipt to all disabled retirees, so earned retired pay and disability compensation are received in full.
  • Support full implementation of the PACT Act to ensure veterans exposed to toxic substances receive timely and complete benefits.
  • Expand access to Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy (HBOT) for treatment of persistent TBI and PTSD/PTSI.
  • Strengthen suicide prevention efforts through research, treatment access, and caregiver support.
  • Ensure women veterans receive equitable access to high-quality, gender-specific care.
  • Ensure veterans have access to emerging and alternative therapies, including psychedelic-assisted treatments, under strict medical supervision with full informed consent, safety protocols, and evidence-based evaluation.
  • Guarantee veterans' right to informed consent across all treatment options, with clear disclosure of risks, benefits, alternatives, and the voluntary nature of participation in any medical or therapeutic intervention.
Focus Group 3

Military Healthcare

Protect access, affordability, and readiness of the military health system.

  • Preserve earned TRICARE benefits and oppose fee increases and higher pharmacy copays.
  • Expand access to healthcare benefits to keep pace with the private sector and other federal employees.
  • Ensure any military medical billet reductions or MTF restructuring do not reduce access or shift costs onto families.
  • Allow pre-tax payment of health, dental, and long-term care premiums to reduce out-of-pocket burden.
  • Expand access to TRICARE Reserve Select, including for Air Reserve Technicians.
Focus Group 4

Military Families

Reduce systemic stressors that harm readiness and retention.

  • Expand access to affordable, high-quality childcare aligned with operational realities.
  • Strengthen military spouse employment programs and eliminate PCS-driven licensure barriers.
  • Promote policies that accommodate military lifestyles, including tax and payment flexibility.
  • Preserve Impact Aid to ensure military-connected children receive consistent educational support.
Focus Group 5

Education

Protect and modernize education benefits for service members and families.

  • Fully fund MilTA, MyCAA, and GI Bill programs and preserve benefit transferability.
  • Ensure all components of the Total Force have equitable access to federal tuition assistance.
  • Improve credit transfer and prior learning recognition to reduce time-to-degree and cost.
  • Focus on empowering the Airman for future success before, during, and after transition.
Focus Group 6

Caring for Survivors

Ensure survivors are not penalized for their sacrifice.

  • Allow surviving spouses to receive the full final month of retired pay.
  • Permit DIC recipients to remarry at age 55 without loss of benefits.
  • Protect survivor benefits from offsets or erosion under budgetary pressures.
Focus Group 7

Air National Guard & Air Force Reserve

Deliver benefit equity without reducing compensation value.

  • Credit all post-9/11 active-duty service toward Guard and Reserve early retirement.
  • Reduce the retirement age and remove caps on creditable inactive duty points.
  • Expand education benefits for Guard members and dual-status technicians.
  • Reform duty status authorities to ensure compensation, benefits, and protections match service performed.
Platform Spotlight · Education

Securing the Promise: GI Bill Transferability

Active Advocacy — Before Congress Now

The Education plank calls on Congress to preserve benefit transferability — and this is where that plank meets the current fight. AFSA is championing bipartisan reform to modernize the Post-9/11 GI Bill's transfer rules so they keep pace with how military families actually grow. A benefit earned in uniform shouldn't expire at the front gate.

How We Got Here — The Education Record

1997
Education Breakthrough

100 percent Tuition Assistance authorized, and Montgomery GI Bill enrollment opened to VEAP-era members.

2008
The Post-9/11 GI Bill

Full state college tuition plus housing and book stipends — and GI Bill transferability to spouses and children secured for the first time.

2017
The "Forever" GI Bill

Lifetime benefit with no expiration, Purple Heart eligibility, expanded transferability, and STEM assistance.

2026The Fight Continues
Transferability Reform

AFSA is working with Congress to finish the job — updating transfer rules so the families of those who served aren't left behind by a paperwork deadline.

The Problem

Under current law, GI Bill benefits can only be transferred while still serving — with a four-year additional service obligation. Members who separate or retire before transferring, or who welcome children later in life, lose the option forever, no matter how many years they served.

The Fix

Allow eligible members and veterans to transfer earned benefits to a spouse or child at any time — during service or after separation — including dependents added to the family after retirement, so no one is penalized for when their family grows.

Why It Matters

Transferability was built as a retention tool — but the rules haven't kept pace with military family life. Enlisted families disproportionately grow later in a career and lose out. Fairness means the benefit follows the service, not the paperwork deadline.

AFSA is engaging members of Congress and testifying alongside The Military Coalition to move transferability reform this session. Legislative avenues include the bipartisan Military Family GI Bill Promise Act (H.R. 4540), alongside companion efforts to open transfer eligibility at ten years of service and extend benefits to foster children and legal wards.
